Research Project

Gaming Use, Problematic Gaming, and Depressive Symptoms in Children and Adolescents

Manuscript under revision · Healthcare

Three-Wave Cohort Study with Statistical and Machine-Learning Analyses

Examined the associations of gaming use patterns and problematic gaming symptoms with depressive symptoms among children and adolescents using a large school-based cohort.

Applied mixed-effects modeling to repeated-measures data and complementary machine-learning analyses to evaluate concurrent classification of elevated depressive symptoms.

Developed participant-level nested cross-validation procedures for Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and XGBoost, including hyperparameter optimization and threshold selection while preventing information leakage across repeated observations.

Key finding

Problematic gaming symptoms showed consistent associations with depressive symptoms at both the between-person and within-person levels, whereas gaming time showed substantially weaker associations after accounting for problematic gaming symptoms.
